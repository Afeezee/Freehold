# Contributor Licence Agreement

Freehold is released under the AGPL-3.0, and a separate commercial licence
is offered to people who cannot accept the copyleft obligation. Offering
both requires one person to hold the rights to the whole codebase. Once a
contribution is merged under the AGPL alone, nobody — including the
maintainer — can include it in a commercially licensed copy without going
back and asking its author. In practice that becomes impossible as soon as
a contributor changes email address or loses interest.

So contributions need this agreement. It is short, and it is the whole of
it.

## What you agree to

By submitting a contribution (a pull request, a patch, or code in an
issue), you confirm that:

1. **You wrote it, or you have the right to submit it.** It is your own
   work, or you have permission from whoever owns it — including your
   employer, if the work was done on their time or equipment.

2. **You grant a licence to use it in both forms.** You give the project
   maintainer a perpetual, worldwide, irrevocable, royalty-free licence to
   use, reproduce, modify and distribute your contribution, including as
   part of a commercially licensed copy of Freehold.

3. **You keep your copyright.** This is a licence, not an assignment. Your
   contribution stays yours, and you may use it anywhere else you like.

4. **You grant a patent licence** covering any patent claims you hold that
   your contribution would otherwise infringe, on the same terms.

5. **No warranty is implied.** Your contribution is provided as is.

## How to sign

Add a line to the end of each commit message:

```
Signed-off-by: Your Name <your@email.example>
```

`git commit -s` adds it for you. That sign-off means you agree to the
above, and to the [Developer Certificate of Origin](https://developercertificate.org/).

## If you would rather not

That is a reasonable position, and some people hold it on principle
because clause 2 lets the maintainer earn money from work you gave away.
Two alternatives that need no agreement:

- **Open an issue** describing the problem and how you would fix it. A
  described bug is worth a great deal and carries no licensing question.
- **Fork it.** The AGPL gives you that right unconditionally, and nothing
  here takes it away.
